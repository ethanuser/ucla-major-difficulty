Adjusting for Student Ability

The primary rankings measure observed grading difficulty — how grades actually distribute in each major's required courses. However, major GPAs partly reflect the ability of students who select into those majors, not just grading strictness. This page explains the rough adjustment we offer using transfer-admit GPA data.

Why adjust?

A low average GPA in a major could mean (a) the courses are graded strictly, or (b) the students who choose that major happen to be less prepared, or (c) some mix. Without individual transcript data (which is protected by FERPA), we can't disentangle these effects perfectly. But we can use a proxy for student ability to do a rough correction.

The proxy: transfer-admit GPA

UCLA publishes the Transfer Admission Profile for each major. This includes 25th and 75th percentile GPA of admitted transfer students. We use the midpoint as a rough "ability proxy" for each major.

Limitation: Transfer-admit GPA reflects community college performance, not UCLA student ability. It also conflates student ability with pre-transfer course difficulty — earning a 4.0 in pre-CS coursework is harder than a 3.95 in pre-Econ. Many majors hit a ceiling near 4.00, compressing variance. This is a rough robustness check, not a causal estimate.

The model

We run a simple OLS regression: course_gpa ~ ability_proxy. The key finding is that majors with higher-ability admits tend to have lower course GPAs — consistent with STEM departments grading more strictly even when their students are stronger.

adjusted_gpa = raw_gpa − k × (ability_proxy − mean_ability)

Where k = |slope| from the OLS fit. This penalizes majors with higher-GPA admits (making their effective GPA lower) and helps majors with lower-GPA admits. In effect, CS and engineering majors rise in the adjusted rankings because their high admit GPAs suggest their low course GPAs reflect genuinely strict grading.
